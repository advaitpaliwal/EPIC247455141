# Two Small Planet Candidates at 3.07 and 5.46 Days around the M Dwarf EPIC 247455141 from K2 Campaign 13

Advait Paliwal
Independent researcher
advaitpaliwal@gmail.com

## Abstract

I report two transit-like signals on the M dwarf EPIC 247455141 (TIC 118598568; 147 pc) in K2 Campaign 13. A joint fit gives periods of 3.07485 and 5.45573 d, depths of 1091 ± 97 and 892 (+97/−99) ppm, and radii of 1.30 ± 0.09 and 1.16 ± 0.08 R⊕. With Gaia companion limits, TRICERATOPS gives FPP = 0.0139 ± 0.0021 and 0.0138 ± 0.0017. No high-resolution imaging exists, so I make no validation claim. A third signal at 9.358 d is an unvetted candidate signal. The star is not a K2 candidate, TOI or CTOI.

## Star and search

EPIC 247455141 has Kp = 15.29, Teff = 3550 ± 157 K, R★ = 0.391 ± 0.012 R☉ and M★ = 0.381 ± 0.021 M☉ (Stassun et al. 2019). Its Gaia DR3 parallax is 6.794 ± 0.040 mas and its RUWE 1.03, with no non-single-star solution (Gaia Collaboration et al. 2023). The nearest Gaia source is 18.9″ away (G = 17.48, against 15.56).

I searched EVEREST light curves (Luger et al. 2018) of 3,000 cool dwarfs at 138–159 pc observed by K2 (Howell et al. 2014), selected from Hardegree-Ullman et al. (2020), with wotan (Hippke et al. 2019; biweight, 0.4 d window) and Transit Least Squares (TLS; Hippke & Heller 2019) over 0.5–30 d. Detections had to match in EVEREST, K2SFF (Vanderburg & Johnson 2014) and both campaign halves and pass odd–even, secondary, duration and neighbour tests. TLS found 3.0752 d (SDE 23.4, SNR 12.8) and 5.4560 d (SDE 19.3, SNR 9.9).

With both masked, TLS finds a 9.356 d signal (SDE 20.1, nine transits, fitted depth 1339 ppm). Its FPP is 0.072 ± 0.006 (no run below 0.035), K2SFF does not recover it, and its duration matches the TIC density only at impact parameter 0.73. I fit it only so it does not bias b and c.

## Transit fit

The EVEREST light curve (29.4-minute cadence, 2017 March 8 to May 27) was detrended with a 0.75 d wotan biweight, all three signals masked and the trend interpolated across them. I fitted the three signals jointly with batman (Kreidberg 2015) and emcee (Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013), integrating over each exposure, with circular orbits, one stellar density shared by all three under a TIC prior, and uninformative quadratic limb darkening (Kipping 2013). The fit gives (Figure 1):

- b: P = 3.074847 (+0.000242/−0.000245) d, T0 = 2457859.8542 ± 0.0018 BJD_TDB, Rp/R★ = 0.0306 ± 0.0020, impact parameter 0.42 (+0.16/−0.24), T14 = 1.34 (+0.10/−0.12) h, Rp = 1.30 ± 0.09 R⊕, insolation 24.2 (+4.6/−4.0) S⊕, Teq = 617 ± 27 K.
- c: P = 5.455730 (+0.000549/−0.000580) d, T0 = 2457861.2849 ± 0.0025 BJD_TDB, Rp/R★ = 0.0273 ± 0.0018, impact parameter 0.28 ± 0.18, T14 = 1.70 (+0.06/−0.12) h, Rp = 1.16 ± 0.08 R⊕, insolation 11.3 (+2.1/−1.9) S⊕, Teq = 510 ± 23 K.

Teq assumes zero albedo and full redistribution. A fit of b and c alone changes no parameter by more than 0.08σ. Free densities for b and c (8.5 (+4.1/−3.6), 6.7 (+2.7/−2.6) ρ☉) match the TIC 6.39 ρ☉.

## Vetting

About their weighted mean, the EVEREST per-transit depths give χ² = 40.7 for 24 degrees of freedom (b) and 26.2 for 14 (c). With only b and c masked, 16% and 26% of 300 transit-free trial epochs match the reduced χ² (1.54, 1.61). Odd and even depths differ by 1.7σ (b) and 0.8σ (c), matched by 14% and 47% of 1000 trial epochs. Depths at phase 0.5 are −83 ± 69 and −11 ± 78 ppm. Box depths in K2SFF and my own aperture photometry (b: 953 ± 134, 1220 ± 123 ppm; c: 1030 ± 148, 846 ± 140 ppm) agree with EVEREST (1006 ± 80, 856 ± 91 ppm) within 1.5σ.

Difference-image centroids lie 1.2″ (b) and 0.7″ (c) from the out-of-transit centroid; 77% and 87% of 60 difference images without a transit show larger offsets, as do 80% for K2-133 b (Wells et al. 2018), a known planet in the same campaign.

No high-resolution imaging exists in ExoFOP or the Keck, Gemini, ESO and MAST archives. TESS observed the star in Sectors 43, 44 and 71 (T = 14.46). My Sector 43 extraction gives a b depth of 2403 ± 544 ppm that the QLP light curve does not reproduce (−3882 ± 2630 ppm), so I do not use TESS.

## False-positive probability

I averaged TRICERATOPS (Giacalone et al. 2021) in its K2 mode, with all seven stars within 40″ kept, over ten runs of 10⁶ draws, using the two-planet-fit depths and bound-companion limits from Gaia RUWE and contrast via MOLUSC (Wood et al. 2021). The results are FPP = 0.0139 ± 0.0021 (range 0.0107–0.0176) for b and 0.0138 ± 0.0017 (0.0111–0.0165) for c, with NFPP = 0 in every run. Without companion limits they are 0.072 and 0.065. Most of the remainder is a planet orbiting an unresolved bound companion. Candidates in multi-candidate systems are less often false positives (Lissauer et al. 2012); I apply no multiplicity factor.

## Summary

EPIC 247455141 b and c are 1.30 and 1.16 R⊕ planet candidates (period ratio 1.774), not listed in the NASA Exoplanet Archive, ExoFOP or SIMBAD as of 2026 October 13. High-resolution imaging and ground-based photometry are the next steps. Code and data are at https://github.com/advaitpaliwal/EPIC247455141.
